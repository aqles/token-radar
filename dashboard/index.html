How the radar prices a launch

Implied value = TVL × peer multiple × size multiplier × saturation multiplier. The peer multiple is the median market-cap-to-TVL of the token-bearing protocols in the same category, computed from this snapshot — never typed in. A category with exactly two trading peers gets a log-space blend of its own median and the global median, capped at twice the global median. A category with fewer than two peers falls back to the global median: one peer is not a category signal. The size multiplier comes from a fitted curve: log(mcap) = 6.9646 + 0.5223·log(TVL), fit by ordinary least squares on the 128 traded protocols in this snapshot. The curve's own multiple is clamped to [0.5×, 2×] of the peer multiple before it is applied. The clamp keeps the top rows credible. It is a prior, not a fit — the unbounded curve is the better held-out predictor, and the evidence strip above says so. Value saturates at $500M TVL (radar-7): a row above $500M is priced as if it held $500M, so Gauntlet, Nonce Capital, Sentora and Steakhouse Financial tie at $53,450,815, each labelled with its saturation multiplier. The old top-5 guardrail (multiples at or above ~0.08×) is retired above $500M TVL: traded giants here trade 0.0006–0.352×, median ~0.03× — Sentora at 0.019× sits between the 25th percentile and the median for its size band.

Token odds are a fitted logistic model, not a category rate: logit(p) = logit(shrunk category rate) − 0.0182 − 0.154·z(log TVL) − 0.485·z(7-day growth) + 0.312·z(log chains), fit by gradient descent (500 iterations) on all 758 protocols at run time. The offset is the shrunk category rate: the share of the category's protocols that already have a token, shrunk toward the snapshot-wide 19% with a prior of ten protocols. Age is deliberately excluded: the chance a protocol has a token rises with age mechanically — older protocols have had more time to launch — which says nothing about whether an old protocol with no token yet will launch next. Expected value is the implied launch value times the token odds. Every farm plan is costed — capital plus gas — and the detail view shows the sized deposit, the break-even deposit and the verdict at that size, so the verdict is a number, not a feeling.

Momentum, maturity and chain breadth were tested held-out and removed: with them the model predicts traded market caps with a median error of 4.20×; without them, 3.96× — so the simpler peer × size curve prices the board, and the three multipliers ship as 1.00 in every row. Within-category size slopes were tested and rejected: they fail their own shuffle control. Scored as a model, the odds hold up on 4-fold splits: Brier 0.1502 / AUC 0.650, against 0.1546 / 0.600 for the shrunk category rate and 0.1553 / 0.500 for the flat 19% rate — the fitted odds beat both baselines where the test is a random split.

Rank ranges come from 200 bootstrap refits of the fitted model: the 128 traded comparables are resampled with replacement (seed 20261122), the size curve and the peer medians are refit, and every row is re-priced and re-ranked. Each row shows the P10–P90 range and the median of its rank across the refits — a stability statement. Median width 37 ranks over the board; the top 3 rows are 21, 12, 5 ranks wide.

The second held-out test is a listing-date cohort check: the cap2 model (peer multiple × peer-bounded size curve, [0.5×, 2×] clamp) is fit on dated comparables listed before 2023 / 2024 / 2025 and tested on those listed after. Fit on 24 protocols (listed before 2023-01-01), test on 71 (listed after 2023-01-01): median error 3.22× vs peer 5.07×, closer 69.0% of the time. Fit on 44 (before 2024-01-01), test on 51 (after): 2.83× vs 5.16×, closer 72.5%. Fit on 64 (before 2025-01-01), test on 31 (after): 2.85× vs 4.91×, closer 71.0%. Two rolling-origin splits agree (2.76× vs 6.01×; 3.13× vs 4.20×, closer 70.8% both). Listing-date check: fitting on protocols listed before 2023 / 2024 / 2025 and testing on those listed after gives median error 3.22x / 2.83x / 2.85x (closer than the peer median 69-73% of the time). This is a cohort split, not a historical backtest: it shows the model generalises to newer listings, not that it would have predicted prices at the time. The headline held-out number remains the leave-one-out median error, 3.94x.

How it's built. The size curve is fitted by ordinary least squares on the 128 traded protocols in this snapshot; the peer multiples are medians from the same snapshot, never typed in. Held out, the adopted model predicts traded market caps with a median error of 3.94× against 5.53× for the peer multiple alone, and the listing-date cohort check agrees at 3.22× / 2.83× / 2.85× against peer 5.07× / 5.16× / 4.91×. The token odds are scored on 4-fold splits: Brier 0.1502 / AUC 0.650, against 0.1553 / 0.500 for the flat 19% rate. Every row carries a costed farm plan with a solved break-even deposit and a verdict at the sized deposit. The sized deposit is twice the break-even deposit, capped at ten times the plan deposit — so every FARM row carries a positive expected net at its sized deposit. The page re-derives every implied value and every token odds from data.js at load: 9/9 checks pass. The ledger accounts for all 758 protocols in the snapshot: 194 ranked + 148 excluded with a reason each + 416 with a token.

Implied value is this entry's own claim from the snapshot's figures. It is not a prediction that a token will be issued, or when, or at what price. A protocol is called pre-token only because its record carries no token — its record may still miss one. Stablecoins are not launch candidates: their token already exists and trades, pegged. They are left out of the early radar; the two on the watchlist stay on the board, flagged, because the watchlist is the floor.
