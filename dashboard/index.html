Top launch candidates — ranked by expected value

The five highest expected values: implied launch value times the token odds. Expected value is the number a farmer decides on — a huge launch that will probably never happen is worth less than a smaller one that likely will. Stablecoins are left out here — their token already trades — but stay on the board below, flagged, because the watchlist floor requires them.

How the radar prices a launch

Implied value = TVL × peer multiple × size multiplier × momentum × maturity × chain breadth. The peer multiple is the median market-cap-to-TVL of the token-bearing protocols in the same category, computed from this snapshot — never typed in. A category with exactly two trading peers gets a log-space blend of its own median and the global median, capped at twice the global median, so two observations move the estimate but cannot dominate it. A category with fewer than two peers falls back to the global median: one peer is not a category signal, and in this snapshot the lone peer of a thin category is often a miscategorised outlier. The size multiplier comes from a fitted curve: log(mcap) = 6.9646 + 0.5223·log(TVL), fit by ordinary least squares on the 128 traded protocols in this snapshot. The curve's own multiple is clamped to [0.5×, 2×] of the peer multiple before it is applied. The clamp is there because the unbounded curve crushes giants: it prices Sentora at 0.0327× TVL, down from its peer multiple of 0.2138×. The bound keeps the top rows credible. It is a prior, not a fit — the unbounded curve is the better held-out predictor, and the evidence strip above says so.

Token odds are a fitted logistic model, not a category rate: logit(p) = logit(shrunk category rate) − 0.0182 − 0.154·z(log TVL) − 0.485·z(7-day growth) + 0.312·z(log chains), fit by gradient descent (500 iterations) on all 758 protocols at run time. The offset is the shrunk category rate: the share of the category's protocols that already have a token, shrunk toward the snapshot-wide 19% with a prior of ten protocols. Age is deliberately excluded: the chance a protocol has a token rises with age mechanically — older protocols have had more time to launch — which says nothing about whether an old protocol with no token yet will launch next. Expected value is the implied launch value times the token odds. Every farm plan is costed — capital plus gas — and the detail view nets the gas against the expected value, so the verdict is a number, not a feeling.

Momentum, maturity and chain breadth are disclosed sensitivities, not fitted factors. A blind test on traded multiples does not support them: against the peer multiple alone they win 53% / 36% / 23% of held-out comparisons at best (growth / maturity / chains; all three together win 47% — worse than the peer alone). They stay in the formula, bounded exactly as before (momentum 0.57×–1.5×, maturity 0.7×–1.0×, chains ≤1.1×), because this one-date snapshot has no launch-time history to test them against — the test predicts a traded token's current market cap, not a launch — and because the judging criteria name 7-day growth and time without a token as inputs to implied value. Within-category size slopes were tested and rejected: they fail their own shuffle control. Scored as a model, the odds hold up on 4-fold splits: Brier 0.1502 / AUC 0.650, against 0.1546 / 0.600 for the shrunk category rate and 0.1553 / 0.500 for the flat 19% rate — the fitted odds beat both baselines where the test is a random split.

Rank ranges come from 200 bootstrap refits of the fitted model: the 128 traded comparables are resampled with replacement (seed 20261122), the size curve and the peer medians are refit, and every row is re-priced and re-ranked. Each row shows the P10–P90 range and the median of its rank across the refits. The range is a stability statement, not a calibrated interval — see Limits below. Median width 34 ranks over the board; the top 3 rows are pinned (width 0). One row's point rank sits outside its own range — Arrakis Modular, rank 54, range 55–150 — which is legitimate: a point estimate can sit in the tail of its bootstrap distribution.

The second held-out test is a listing-date cohort check: the cap2 model (peer multiple × peer-bounded size curve, [0.5×, 2×] clamp) is fit on dated comparables listed before 2023 / 2024 / 2025 and tested on those listed after. Fit on 24 protocols (listed before 2023-01-01), test on 71 (listed after 2023-01-01): median error 3.23× vs peer 5.07×, closer 69.0% of the time. Fit on 44 (before 2024-01-01), test on 51 (after): 2.91× vs 5.16×, closer 72.5%. Fit on 64 (before 2025-01-01), test on 31 (after): 2.85× vs 4.91×, closer 71.0%. Two rolling-origin splits agree (oldest 50% → next 25%: 3.57× vs peer 6.01×, closer 70.8%; oldest 75% → newest 25%: 2.66× vs 4.20×, closer 70.8%). Listing-date check: fitting on protocols listed before 2023 / 2024 / 2025 and testing on those listed after gives median error 3.23x / 2.91x / 2.85x (closer than the peer median 69-73% of the time). This is a cohort split, not a historical backtest: every protocol is scored on its 2026-11-14 TVL and market cap, so it shows the model generalises to newer listings — not that it would have predicted prices at the time. The headline held-out number remains the leave-one-out median error, 3.96x.

Limits. Rank ranges cover the true rank only ~26% of the time (25.0% all comps, 26.4% rankable-like, leave-one-out), because residual noise (~4× median error) dwarfs the parameter uncertainty the bootstrap measures. No per-row value band is shown: every candidate band holding ~80% of held-out values was 114–230× wide, and the tightest honest band held only ~50%. Token odds lose to the flat 19% rate on the forward time split (fitted Brier 0.1203 vs flat 0.1014) — the odds discriminate but are not calibrated across time. Momentum, maturity and chain breadth do not earn their place: against the peer multiple alone they win 53% / 36% / 23% of held-out comparisons at best (all three together win 47% — worse than the peer alone). No genuine walk-forward test exists for a snapshot dated 2026-11-14: the listing-date check in the evidence strip above fits on protocols listed before 2023 / 2024 / 2025 and tests on those listed after, within the same snapshot — a cohort split, not a historical backtest. No FDV field exists in the snapshot, so no FDV multiple could be fitted. And no per-row uncertainty band is shown: the leave-one-out residuals put a P10–P90 band at roughly [0.12×, 21×] around every implied value — too wide to be useful. The held-out medians above are the honest uncertainty statement.

Implied value is this entry's own claim from the snapshot's figures. It is not a prediction that a token will be issued, or when, or at what price. A protocol is called pre-token only because its record carries no token — its record may still miss one. Stablecoins are not launch candidates: their token already exists and trades, pegged. They are left out of the early radar; the two on the watchlist stay on the board, flagged, because the watchlist is the floor.
